  simulated a smoothing fix. Here's what I found and the plan I'd suggest.

  What capture.csv shows

  - Speech gets classified as keywords: 245 of 621 windows (40%) have a keyword
    as their top prediction, and the label changes 166 times in 62 s. The model
    is unsure almost everywhere: the median top probability is only 0.45.
  - Long silences are mostly handled correctly: the 10–20 s and 52–62 s pauses

  I think there are four causes, roughly in order of impact:

  1. The model has never seen continuous speech or partial words. Every training
     example is one isolated word, roughly centred, surrounded by near-silence.
     In your speech, a window holding the end of one word and the start of
     another looks more like a keyword than anything it saw labelled other.
     Also, other is cut down to 20% of the data, so the model rarely sees
     non-keyword speech.
  2. There's no augmentation. augment_clip() in the notebook does nothing, so
     there's no time shift, no mixed-in noise and no gain variation.
  3. The background class is mostly synthetic noise. 1,140 of the 1,478 clips
     are blue, brown or violet noise at about −14 dBFS. There's no quiet room
     tone, no background chatter or TV, and no music.
  4. The board mic sounds different from the dataset:
     - Its noise floor during pauses is about −45 dBFS, against about −65 dBFS
       in Speech Commands clips.
     - Your speech peaks about 8 dB quieter than the dataset (about −24 vs −15
       dBFS).
     - There's a large DC offset that drifts from −0.33 to −0.07 of full scale.
       That's because the BSP leaves the ADF high-pass filter off
       (HighPassFilter.Activation = DISABLE, b_u585i_iot02a_audio.c:427).

     DC sits below the 125 Hz lowest mel filter, so it only slightly affects the
     features. But it uses up headroom: 2 samples hit full scale and 22 went
     past 0.9, all on the negative side.

  A correction to what I said earlier: I told you there was no normalisation.
  There is: it happens inside the C library, which subtracts LOG_MEL_MEAN and
  divides by LOG_MEL_STDDEV. All the scripts call the library, so nothing
  changes.

  Smoothing alone helps, but isn't enough. I applied TF Micro-style
  post-processing to your capture: average the outputs, require a threshold,
  then ignore new detections for 1.5 s. With 0.5 s averaging and a 0.7
  threshold, 245 keyword windows reduce to 12 detections:

  What others do

  - Google's Speech Commands training script (train.py
    (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tensorflow/tensorflow/v2.4.0/tensorflow/e
    xamples/speech_commands/train.py)) mixes background noise into 80% of
    samples at up to 0.1 volume. It shifts samples randomly by up to ±100 ms and
    adds 10% silence and 10% unknown words. Your pipeline currently has none of
    these.
  - Wake-word projects built for streaming audio (microWakeWord
    (https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/ohf-voice/micro-wake-word),
    openWakeWord (https://github.com/alfiedennen/openwakeword-colab-2026)):
    - They train on very large amounts of negative audio, about 2,000 hours of
      general audio in openWakeWord's case.
    - They use roughly 10–20 negative windows per positive.
    - Augmentation includes room reverb (MIT room impulse responses), noise and
      music from MUSAN, FSD50K and WHAM!, and pitch and speed changes.
    - They add confusable words as extra negatives, and often use positive
      samples generated with Piper text-to-speech.
    - They measure false accepts per hour on long audio, not per-clip accuracy.
    - The reason that metric matters: with 10 windows per second, even a 5%
      per-window false-positive rate is about 1,800 false alarms an hour
      (https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/MJ%20CAREY%20ES%20PARRIS
      %20IMPROVEMENTS%20TO%20A%20KEYWORD%20RECOGNITION%20ALGORITHM.pdf).
  - TF Micro's RecognizeCommands (source
    (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tensorflow/tensorflow/v2.4.0/tensorflow/l
    ite/micro/examples/micro_speech/recognize_commands.h)) averages outputs over
    1000 ms, needs a score of 200/255 (about 0.78), suppresses repeats for 1500
    ms and needs at least 3 results in the average.
  - Datasets:
    - For continuous speech: LibriSpeech (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24820) (960
      h).
    - For many more isolated words: the Multilingual Spoken Words Corpus
      (https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/fe131d
      7f5a6b38b23cc967316c13dae2-Abstract-round2.html) (23 M one-second words).
    - For noise: MUSAN, FSD50K or ESC-50.
  - Edge Impulse recommends
    (https://docs.edgeimpulse.com/docs/responding-to-your-voice) recording on
    the target device itself, with at least 10 minutes per class including noise
    and "unknown".

  Is it worth recording your own data on the board?

  Yes. Minute for minute, board recordings are your most valuable data, because
  they're the only data with your mic's noise floor, level and DC behaviour. In
  priority order:

  1. Negatives: the cheapest and most useful. Use receive_and_play.py -o and
     record long stretches of yourself talking or reading aloud, TV and
     podcasts, music, typing, kitchen noise and plain room tone. No labelling is
     needed, since every 1 s window is other or _background_noise_. Aim for
     hours, not minutes.
  2. Keywords in context. Say sentences like "go forward a bit" and "please
     stop", as well as the keywords alone. Vary distance (0.3–2 m), loudness and
     speed. These need timestamps; an Audacity label track exported to the
     _truth.csv format works, and then view_results.py shows it as an overlay.
  3. Other speakers if possible. Otherwise the model risks learning your voice;
     Speech Commands still provides speaker variety.
  4. A held-out test set. Keep some recording sessions out of training entirely
     and use them for evaluation.

  Suggested plan, cheapest first

  0. Measure first. Record a labelled board evaluation set of about 5 minutes:
     speech, occasional keywords and pauses. Then add a score_results.py that
     reports keyword recall and false accepts per minute after smoothing. Every
     later step is judged by these two numbers, not per-clip accuracy.
  1. Add smoothing, a threshold and suppression to the host scripts, and tune
     them on the evaluation set. The firmware will need the same logic later
     anyway.
  2. Add augmentation and better negatives in the notebook. I expect this to be
     the biggest improvement.
     - Time shift of ±100–200 ms.
     - Partial-keyword negatives: windows where only a fragment of a keyword is
       inside, labelled other. This targets the label flipping you're seeing
       directly.
     - 1 s crops of LibriSpeech, skipping utterances whose transcript contains a
       keyword.
     - Use far more of Speech Commands' other words, especially confusable ones
       like follow and four. Weight the classes so keyword recall doesn't
       collapse.
     - Mix real noise into about 80% of samples at 0–20 dB SNR.
     - Random gain of ±12 dB.
     - Rebuild _background_noise_ around real noise and quiet room tone, with
       less synthetic noise.
     - Optionally, room reverb and SpecAugment.

     All of this happens on the waveform before the C preprocessing, so the
     ctypes pipeline stays as it is. To speed it up, run it across several
     processes; each process loads its own copy of the library, so its internal
     state isn't shared.
  3. Add the board recordings from the previous section to training.
  4. Remove the DC offset. Subtracting each frame's mean inside the C library

     Plan: Step 0: labelled board eval set + streaming score (recall / false
     accepts)

     Context

     On board audio the model flip-flops between keywords and other during
     continuous speech. Before
     changing data or augmentation we need a fixed, labelled evaluation set
     recorded on the board mic,
     and a script that turns run_model_on_wav.py output into the two numbers
     every later change is
     judged by: keyword recall and false accepts per minute, after simple
     smoothing, threshold
     and suppression. Decisions so far:
     - Labelling is done in Audacity, with a small converter script.
     - Recordings are committed in HostSW/eval_recordings/.

     Label policy: only keywords (plus an optional ignore)

     - Label only keyword spans (forward, backward, stop). Everything unlabelled
       counts as negative
       audio. Scoring only asks two questions: was each keyword detected, and
       did anything fire where no
       keyword was said? So the other vs _background_noise_ split isn't needed
       and won't be labelled.
     - An extra ignore label marks audio that shouldn't count either way: a
       cut-off keyword,
       "stopped" / "forwards", coughs, or a keyword said by the TV. Detections
       there count as neither
       hits nor false accepts, and that time is excluded from the false-accept
       minutes.
     - A span is the audible word, start to end, as drawn in Audacity. It
       doesn't need to be exact: the
       matching tolerance is about 1 s.

     1. Recording: HostSW/eval_recordings/README.md (protocol) +
     receive_and_play.py -o

     No code changes. The README gives the recording recipe and the labelling
     steps:
     - About 5 min per file, eval_<nn>_<desc>.wav. Target about 10 of each
       keyword per file,
       made up of:
       - isolated keywords with pauses;
       - keywords inside sentences ("go forward a bit", "please stop there");
       - fast and slow speech, and distances of 0.3, 1 and 2 m;
       - confusable non-keywords ("four", "follow", "top", "back", "forwarding";
         label variants like
         these as ignore only when they really are ambiguous);
       - about 30 s of room tone;
       - about 1 min with TV or music playing.
     - Keep the files as a held-out test set: never train on them. Later
       training recordings go
       elsewhere.
     - The existing HostSW/capture.wav can become eval_01_speech.wav once it's
       labelled.

     2. Labelling: Audacity + HostSW/audacity_labels_to_truth.py

     - README steps:
       a. Open the WAV in Audacity. Optionally use the spectrogram view (track
          menu → Spectrogram).
       b. Select a word and press Cmd+B, then type the label.
       c. File → Export → Export Labels → <stem>_labels.txt.
       d. Run the converter.
       e. To edit later: File → Import → Labels.
     - Converter CLI: -i labels.txt (required); -o (default <wav
       stem>_truth.csv, derived from
       labels.txt by replacing a _labels suffix); --reverse (truth CSV →
       Audacity labels, so
       make_test_wav.py truth files can be opened in Audacity too).
     - Parsing:
       - Each line is start\tend\tlabel.
       - Skip lines starting with \ (Audacity's spectral-selection lines) and
         blank lines.
       - Strip and lower-case labels. Exit with an error listing the line number
         for any label not in
         {forward, backward, stop, ignore}.
       - Warn about zero-length or overlapping spans.
       - Sort by start time.
     - Output: the same start_s,end_s,label,file header as make_test_wav.py. The
       file column
       is left empty, so view_results.py's truth overlay works unchanged.

     3. HostSW/score_results.py

     - CLI:
       - -r results.csv (required);
       - -t truth.csv (default <results stem>_truth.csv);
       - --avg 0.5 (smoothing window, s);
       - --threshold 0.7;
       - --suppress 1.5 (s);
       - --tolerance (default 1.0 + avg, s);
       - --sweep (threshold table);
       - -o detections CSV (default <results stem>_detections.csv).
     - detect(results, avg_s, threshold, suppress_s) is a pure function so it's
       easy to reuse
       and port to firmware later:
       - Causal moving average of the probability vectors over the last N =
         round(avg_s / stride)
         windows, the way the device would compute it. Needs at least min(3, N)
         windows, as in TF
         Micro's RecognizeCommands.
       - Fires at window i if the top smoothed class is a keyword, its smoothed
         probability is
         ≥ threshold, and at least suppress_s has passed since the last firing.
       - Detection time = window i's end_s, the moment the device would actually
         have that audio.
     - Matching:
       - A detection of label L hits a truth span (s, e, L) if s ≤ t ≤ e +
         tolerance. The window
         must end after the word starts, and the word must still be in the
         window, plus the averaging
         lag.
       - Each span is hit at most once. Later matches of the same span are
         duplicates, counted as
         false accepts but listed separately.
       - A detection that falls in a span with a different keyword is a
         substitution, counted
         as a false accept.
       - A detection matching an ignore span is dropped.
     - Report:
       - Settings used.
       - Per-keyword and overall recall (hits / spans).
       - False accepts (total, plus how many were substitutions or duplicates),
         per minute and per
         hour. The negative time is the results' audio duration minus keyword
         and ignore spans.
       - Lists of misses (span times) and false accepts (time, label, smoothed
         p), so you can
         jump to them in the viewer.
       - --sweep prints recall and false accepts per minute for thresholds
         0.50–0.95 in steps of
         0.05, at the given avg and suppress.
     - Detections CSV: time_s, label, p, outcome (hit / false_accept /
       substitution / duplicate /
       ignored).
